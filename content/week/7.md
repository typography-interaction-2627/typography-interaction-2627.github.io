## Quick Project 1 Recap

Everyone should have received their individual [*Manuscript*](../project/1.md) feedback and evaluations on Slack. Some through-lines:
<!-- .balance -->

- **Attributions?**

	A number of folks brought in other material that we didn’t discuss in class—whether from previous knowledge, peers, MDN, who knows—and we didn’t see much in the way of [attributions](../syllabus.md#attribution). We think we’ve been clear on this—you **must note this** and explain the usage and your understanding.

	In particular, we did not see *any* links to LLM “conversations,” despite [our guidelines](../syllabus.md#our-ai-guidelines) for their use.

- **Smaller Commits, More Comments**

	We’d like to see better code “hygiene”! Commit smaller sets of changes, much more often, and with more useful messages. It shows us how you are working through things, and makes your code more resilient to errors.

	Likewise, [comment](../topic/html/index.md#comments) your code! We didn’t see enough of this.

- **Don’t Repeat Yourself**

	[D.R.Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_repeat_yourself)! Following from above: in code, any time you’re repeating yourself there is probably a better way! In CSS, duplicate declarations probably mean you can use [grouped selectors](../topic/css/index.md#compound-and-lists-selectorselector-selector-selector), [nesting](../topic/css/index.md#also-native-nesting), reuse [a class](../topic/css/index.md#2-a-class-class-name), define [a variable](../topic/box-model/index.md#defined-as---variable), etc.

	This means leaving time for [*refactoring*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_refactoring) your code—going *back* across your work to consolidate/tidy and make your intent clear. We want you to always think [*systemically*](../topic/box-model/index.md#design-systems)!

- **Readability Fundamentals**

	We had lots of expressive type work! But in this, we often lost sight of [the basics](../topic/typography/index.md#type-size-weight-and-color)—many projects *still* had base paragraph text that was uncomfortable to read—lacking contrast, too-big or too-small, too-short or too-long lines.

	Put yourself in the mind of your audience: is this comfortable to read? The easiest way to do this: actually *read* your page, as it is designed!

- **Attention to Detail**

	In a movie, you don’t want to see a reflection of the cameraman in a mirror or a boom mic in frame. In design, you don’t want to see typos or misused punctuation. It just takes us out of the work, and doesn’t demonstrate *care*.

	Said another way: as a designer, *you* are the attention to detail. That’s really all we are!

- **Slack Habits**

	Always include a link to your updated repo and as *specific* a question(s) as possible—showing us you’ve thought things through first, before asking. Use [threads](https://slack.com/help/articles/115000769927-Use-threads-to-organize-discussions) for this, to keep our main channel understandable.

	And we’ve never heard from about half of you in the chat! We’d like to see more activity overall, and engagement with *each other* there too. Everyone is going through the same stuff! And it’ll only be getting more challenging.

	Last: when we [ask for acknowledgement](https://mpscd.slack.com/archives/C0BRNBPC6QP/p1790457709940359) that you’ve read something, we expect acknowledgement! It is on you to mind your notifications and stay on top of our communications.
