## Project 2 Recap

We’ll be sending your [*Binding*](../project/2.md) feedback/evaluations on Slack, either after class or tomorrow. Following [our previous recap](7.md#project-1-recap), some more overall notes:

- **LLM Use**

	- We received *Chat* “conversation” transcripts/links from under half of our groups. We’re assuming the use is (probably much) more widespread—but think we have a feeling for the tone/tenor. (Be sure to “share” your conversations!)

	- When folks use it *with* our `AGENTS.md` file—whether in-IDE, or when *Claude*/whatever has loaded the project—the context/guidelines keep the conversations mostly “on the rails.” This is why we provide these!

	- When used *without* these instructions—so in their own app, or in a browser session—the LLM&NoBreak;s are often (mostly?) leading us astray.

	- We saw a lot of *very long* conversations, too. First, these are hard for us to follow. Second, they make the quality of the responses worse, likely because we’re hitting [*context window*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Context*window) limits—how many tokens the LLM can “hold” at once.

	- Let’s think also about how these tools, as software *products*, might encourage this—they are incentivized to *keep you chatting*. It feels like we are often not in control, just “along for the ride.”

	- Also, if we’re (probably) using the free/low-fee tiers—the quality of the answers is worse. The fanciest models are *extremely* expensive to run. They are also incentivized to give you the minimum they can. (We know the difference; you don’t yet.)

	- The result of this is that we think many of you are wasting *tons* of time using these tools, going back-and-forth with them. And we think that this is actually taking *much* longer, and is definitely more frustrating, than “the old-fashioned way”: simply reading our topic pages, talking with each other, asking us questions in Slack, etc.

	- And even if this doesn’t feel like a waste of time—absent our instructions, the LLM&NoBreak;s also do not have the *context* of our course, its goals, and our methodologies. And these are ultimately what we are here for.

	- The output *does not matter* if you don’t understand it. And we don’t think these tools—without guidelines, going long, using the basic tiers, not knowing our goals—are helping us build our own understanding.

	- So we’ll likely be re-adjusting our approach for Project 3, and will talk about this more in coming weeks. In the meantime, we’ve added some additional points to [our guidelines](../syllabus.md#our-ai-guidelines):

		1. If using an LLM without access to our instructions, the student must “load” (copy and paste) them in first. Every “conversation” should begin with them.

		1. These “conversations” should be kept short, and focus on specific lines of questioning. We do not want long, open-ended back-and-forths. Start new threads when you have a new question.

		1. The LLM should not be asked to provide or “write” our attributions. The explanations are *your* understanding.
		<!-- .bold -->

	- And to reiterate: the point of our guidelines is not to “catch” or punish you, or something! We’re trying to facilitate your learning and understanding—of a complicated subject in a confusing web/world. Let us help!

	<!-- style="row-gap: calc(2rlh - 1rcap)" -->

- **“Show, Don’t Tell”**

	- Many folks will be receiving some form of this feedback—we had a lot of “telling” and generally more “scripted”-feeling introductions/discussions. This makes it harder for our audience to feel engaged with our work.

	- We want to get better at talking about our work, and developing the “muscle” for explaining it “on the fly.” A more conversational, natural delivery will help our ideas/concepts “land” (stick) with our audience.

	- The best way to do this is, of course, *practice*—with other people! The first time you are talking to someone about your work should probably not be in front of us.

	- To prep yourself for Project 3: we will be “driving” these—opening them up, clicking and scrolling around, etc. How will you convey the context you need to while we are doing this?

- **Previous Feedback**

	- Also, lots of folks had unaddressed/unconsidered feedback—whether that we provided individually, or in our previous [recap notes](7.md#project-1-recap). We want to see these considered, of course.

	- We’re all here to *improve*—and these are things we’ve identified where we think you have room to grow!

	- So looking ahead to Project 3, we want you to keep these in mind as you *start* your work, and then *throughout* the process—don’t just leave them for the end (or forget about them entirely).

- **Refactoring / “Tidying Up”**

	- In our implementation, we need to leave time for “circling back around” on our code—another pass to identify patterns, consolidate relationships, make our *intent* more clear.

	- We also just need to have time for “tidying up”—checking our links, adding comments, expanding our README, and so on.

	- Sometimes, this means nothing even visually changes in our work! But *often* it will end up bringing more cohesion and rhythm, as we hone our design systems.

	- We don’t want you to think of these efforts as just busywork—we are practicing doing this, so we internalize it and do it automatically/unconsciously the next time. Like anything else, the *practice* is what is important here!

	- Last, and we’ve said this before—we don’t just want “more” things added, up until our due date. All else equal: fewer things, done better, and showing us that you *understand* them more.

## And a Quick Demo

It’s going to be quick on time, but we want to “connect the dots” today, going through some of the particulars of sketching in code and putting multiple pages together into a *website*. Our agenda:
<!-- .balance -->

1. Head over to our next template repository:

	[<cite>`binding` Template</cite>](https://github.com/typography-interaction-2627/binding)

1. [Create a new repo](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/creating-and-managing-repositories/creating-a-repository-from-a-template) from it, as we [did](3.md#html-time) [before](8.md#working-in-pairs)—keeping the `binding` name, set to <samp>Public</samp>!

1. Clone it down locally and open the `index.html`
	- Note that we’ve already pulled in our reset, now under `styles/reset.css`
	- Also create a `common.css` there with some basic styles/[size variables](../topic/box-model/index.md#defined-as---variable), linking it in
	- Commit/push, then enable [Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/configuring-a-publishing-source-for-your-github-pages-site) and add the link to the repo’s *About* section

1. Let’s do some… *LLM&NoBreak;-ing*?

	- In VS Code, ask *Copilot* a question
	- What makes a good/bad question?
	- Start a thread when we have a new question
	- In a browser “conversation,” give it our `AGENTS.md` for context

2. Make a subpage and stylesheet for sketching

	- Create a folder `direction-1/` with its own `index.html` and sibling `style.css`
	- Link the HTML to the project’s reset, the root/common stylesheet, and also its own
	- With [relative URL&NoBreak;s](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Howto/Web_mechanics/What_is_a_URL#absolute_urls_vs._relative_urls:~:text=All%20of%20the%20following%20URLs%20are%20relative%20URLs%3A): `../` means “go up a level”—so our direction points at the reset with `href="../styles/reset.css"`
	- Do this in *the cascade* order, [“large to small”](../topic/css/index.md#avoiding-these-problems): reset, common, then direction!
	- Copy in some plain, semantic DOM [from your first project](https://github.com/typography-interaction-2627/manuscript/blob/main/index.html)
	- Also add a link back to your landing page `href="../"`, somewhere handy up top

3. Duplicate this *three* times for your *distinct* typographic treatments

	- Copy the `direction-1/` folder so you have `direction-2/` and `direction-3/` as well
	- Change something in each individual direction `style.css`, to easily tell them apart
	- Back on the landing/root `index.html`, make a `<ul>` with links to the directions
	- Since they are children, `href="direction-1/"` will go *down* to that folder (and request its `index.html`)
	- You can keep some basic/shared styles up in `../styles/common.css` (remember, [D.R.Y.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_repeat_yourself))
	- Each direction’s own `style.css` can then be used to sketch their *own* typographic system
