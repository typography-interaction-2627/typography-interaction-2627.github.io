## Notes on Sketches

We’ve gone through your [Figma sketches](https://www.figma.com/files/team/1670950360853186233/folder/649044468) (everyone should have feedback there now) and want to discuss some trends:
<!-- .balance -->

- **Many folks really only had *one* direction. (Maaaybe 1<sup style="margin-inline-end: var(---2px)">1&#x202F;</sup>/<sub>2</sub>, being generous.)**

	A lot of the work landed much closer to “explorations on one theme” rather than “three distinct directions.” Changing the type alone does not make a new direction, nor does a different layout with the same type.

	In addition to not satisfying our [expectations for the assignment](../project/1.md#sketches), this can also only limit your horizons for the project. Sketching is meant to initially *broaden* these, from which we can later narrow. But you can’t refine (and we can’t respond/give feedback to) what we don’t see. We want to see much more varied exploration, here!

- **It also wasn’t very… *sketchy*.**

	Lots of “here are my only three frames.” We want to see more *in-progress* and more iteration/experimentation. These don’t have to be complete thoughts. Sketches are *not* presentations. Be looser. Show us how you got there. Don’t “tidy up” prematurely. Remember, we’re working in the open!

- **Lots of (way) *too-small* or *too-large* type.**

	To *some* extent, scale/zoom doesn’t matter—it is all about the relationships. But we doubt everyone was drawing at 13% or 200% zoom—so the type sizes were pretty tough to parse for their intent.

	Start working at 100% in Figma (<kbd><span class="x2318">⌘</span>/Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>0</kbd></nobr>&#x202F;). Try *actually reading* your text. It is not just a formal element. Always put yourself in the mind/eyes of your reader! And also folks (us and in the future, your boss) reviewing your Figma.

- **Build up from the base (paragraph).**

	Overall, we want to see more of an emphasis on [basic readability](../topic/typography/index.md#type-size-weight-and-color). This is true in any project, but particular one about text and reading! Readability is our “[table stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_stakes);” this requirement precedes all others.

	We’re all designers—everyone here wants to start with their *Bigass Type Moment*, above the fold. But instead—try starting with your most basic, simple, base paragraph. Land that—and it is your typographic “floor”/“foundation” to build *up* from. Think of your larger type moments in *relation* to that base—as increments of its size, or in contrast to its weight.

- **Multi-column, *print* layouts.**

	We left many notes for folks about their multi-column layouts. Columns are a powerful, structural design tool—but are complicated in digital, where the audience only ever sees a portion of your page while scrolling.

	We can’t expect people to scroll down one column, then zigzag back up another. You probably wouldn’t! So don’t expect your reader to, either. Again, put yourself in their mind/eyes.

- **Again, we aren’t designing *posters*.**

	Relatedly, there were still a lot of *Frame-centric* layouts. Keep in mind that on the web, your reader never sees “the whole canvas” as we do in Figma—this is a limitation/weak point of the tool.

	Always think about how your audience will flow (scroll) through your page—especially when the primary purpose is reading, as it is with this project.

	Going further, we didn’t see much in the way of “frameless” work! Remember that with all of our tools, their form and limitations can become our own.

- **More deliberate, intentional design choices—the *why*.**

	Ideally our work should always be bespoke to our content—especially here, where you have chosen it and written in response. Your design should deeply reflect this. Many folks were landing in “default” or “template” territory—as in you could plop *any* text into the design. Consider that an [*anti*-goal](https://awilkinson.medium.com/the-power-of-anti-goals-c38f5f46d23c), here—and really, in your career.

	If the intent (or anything) *isn’t* clear—especially when working asynchronously—you can leave annotations/marginalia to help explain. But the strongest designs stand on their own, of course!

- **More *engagement* (time).**

	Many of these points probably came out of the same root cause: it doesn’t seem to us like a lot of folks spent very long on this! (And Figma timestamped [version histories](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038006754-View-a-file-s-version-history) confirm.)

	This is not an “half-hour before class” course—we need to see more. Let’s course-correct!
<!-- .balance -->

**TL;DR: always mind your medium! And let’s see more engagement, going forward!**

1. Okay, for real, let’s switch gears to CSS. Create a `style.css` in the same folder as your `index.html`.

	<!--- For future you: there is a “zero-width joiner” in the `<style>` here to fix syntax/linting. --->
	- You can click <samp>File</samp> → <samp>New File…</samp>, or click the little <samp>+</samp> icon that hovers over the [<samp>Explorer</samp> pane](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/getstarted/getting-started#_view-and-edit-files-with-the-editor)
	- Recall that CSS [can live](../topic/css/index.md#where-css-lives) also live in a `<‍style>` tag, or even inline—but these don’t scale very well!
	- So we’ll be using an *external* (not in our HTML) stylesheet
	- Great time to talk about VS Code’s [*side-by-side* editing](https://code.visualstudio.com/docs/getstarted/userinterface#_side-by-side-editing)!

1. In the `<head>` of your HTML file, link to your `style.css`:

	```html
	<!doctype html>
	<html>
		<head>
			<title>Your page title</title>
			<link href="style.css" rel="stylesheet">
		</head>
		<body>
			<!-- Your HTML content. -->
		</body>
	</html>
	```

1. It’s always good to add something obvious in, to make sure it is all connected. Add this to your `style.css`:

	```css
	body { color: tomato; }
	```

	If you hop back to your page in the browser, you should see the color change! If not, your HTML isn’t seeing your CSS. Retrace your steps until you see the color—this means we’re “plugged in.”

1. Let’s also try adding in [a reset](../topic/css/index.md#resets) to start with a *clean slate*:

	```html <!-- .all -->
	<link href="https://typography-interaction-2627.github.io/assets/reset.css" rel="stylesheet">
	```

	Try [commenting](../topic/html/index.md#comments) <nobr><kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>/</kbd></nobr> this line in and out, then looking again in the browser! Note the differences, as the *user-agent* styles are reset.

1. We’ll bring this file in locally, to avoid [a *dependency*](https://research.swtch.com/deps#what_is_a_dependency).

	Create a `reset.css` in your repo, as we did with `style.css`. You can go to [the course reset](/assets/reset.css) in your browser, and copy the code into your local file. Then update the `<link>` in your `<head>`:

	```html
	<link href="reset.css" rel="stylesheet">
	```

1. Finally, let’s change up our type!

	- Set a [`font-family`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-family) and [`font-size`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-size) on our `<html>` element
	- You can also target “the whole document” with `:root { }`
	- Font properties [*inherit*](../topic/css/index.md#and-inheritance), but we can set/change/override this at any level/place with different selectors
	- Ahead of next week’s topic, let’s put some space between things with a couple simple rules:

	```css
	body { padding: 3rem; }
	*:where(:not(:first-child)) { margin-block-start: 1em; }
	```

1.	Let’s add something nice from [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/).

	On the [*specimen* page](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/National+Park) (the page for a specific font family), click the <samp>Get font</samp> button—this will add them to a sort of “shopping bag” up top.

	We’ll start by linking to it. Click <samp><> Get embed code</samp> to see our options, choose `<link>`. Select your weights, if necessary (only bring in what you will use), and copy the code there into your `<head>`:

	```html <!-- .all -->
	<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
	<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
	<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=National+Park:wght@200..800&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
	```

	And then we’ll apply it—in this case, to everything:

	```css
	:root { font-family: 'National Park', serif; }
	```

	You can also `@import` right into the top of your stylesheet, if you prefer: <!-- .before--2 .note -->

	```css <!-- .all -->
	@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=National+Park:wght@200..800&display=swap');
	/* The rest of your CSS… */
	```

	The page should now be set in the font!

	The process for [Adobe Fonts](https://helpx.adobe.com/fonts/using/add-fonts-website.html) is somewhat similar, but involves setting up a “project” there first. And know that these fonts (and your design) will go away without a subscription! <!-- .note -->
