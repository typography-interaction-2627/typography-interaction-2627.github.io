# Spread

## Introduction

**Students will now work together—with a new reading from those selected by the class in [*Manuscript*](1.md)—to express a text deliberately and consistently across devices.**

The goal of this project is twofold: both an effective *collaboration* between partners and also a successful, *responsive* design. Each duo will sketch cooperatively and then implement a joint execution together, via pair programming. The final web page will be responsive for mobile, desktop, and print layouts.

## Your Partners

Design is *rarely* practiced entirely on your own—and the same is true for most software development. So collaboration is integral to this work, and is the basic structure for this project—conceptually, visually, and then in code. You’ll be working with a partner:

### Rules for Collaboration

- You are going to be working very closely together for this whole unit, so please be courteous and respectful throughout the process!

- Students will first meet with their partner, exchange necessary contact information, and figure out <nobr>co-working</nobr> times and general availability/approach.

- Students must always work *at the same time*—whether <nobr>in-person</nobr>, on Zoom, in a [Slack huddle](https://slack.com/help/articles/4402059015315-Use-huddles-in-Slack), what have you. This is *not* an asynchronous collaboration. You are not to work separately!

- In Figma, students may sketch on their individual computers but should always be in the same document, at the same time, working *together*.

- In Code, students should work on one computer at a time (for simplicity). One person “drives” with the other watching, and they should switch regularly—maybe every half-hour.

- It does not matter if you’re next to each other at a desk or in a [*Live Share*](https://visualstudio.microsoft.com/services/live-share/) at your homes; these synchronous collaboration rules are the same.

- Each team will have one *shared* project repository for their code, and we should see balanced commits/work from both students. (We will demonstrate working together from one repo.)

- Both students will receive the *same* grade for this project, however it nets out. So it is in everyone’s interest to work together effectively!

Keep in mind that we aren’t looking for some kind of mathematically perfect division, here—all of these things are proxies for us seeing you *collaborate* with your partner. We want a good-faith effort at this.

**You will learn from each other, and your work will be more than what you bring alone!**

## Cooperative Sketching

<div class="body">

You will then sketch *together*, in a single/shared Figma document, to develop your design for this new text. Keep in mind that your design should reflect your combined response to the text—it should be *appropriate* for what you have selected.

As with [our last project](1.md#sketches), include the full reading (or your chapter/excerpt, if it is lengthy) and its title, author, date, and other associated elements. Again any figures, images, or other decoration should be excluded; this is still all about the form of the text itself.

You’ll both work on and refine these *together* into [three discrete directions](../week/4.md#notes-on-sketches)—catalyzed into *distinct*, well-developed design approaches. You have seen your classmates designs for *Manuscript*—but your sketches should be *unquestionably* your own interpretation of the same material. Consider this a constraint; go a different way with your own.

*Each* of your three directions should have their basic mobile, desktop, and print forms sketched out. ## Pair Programming

<div class="body">

As a team, you will decide on a single design direction—likely, a hybrid/composite path—and take this into code.

You’ll again start from a [template repository](https://github.com/typography-interaction-2627/spread), in one person’s GitHub, then [shared](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/setting-up-and-managing-your-personal-account-on-github/managing-access-to-your-personal-repositories/inviting-collaborators-to-a-personal-repository) with their partner. It doesn’t matter who’s GitHub the repo originates/lives in, but both students should be *collaborators*. Make sure this repo is set to <samp>Public</samp> and [enable Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/configuring-a-publishing-source-for-your-github-pages-site), as [before](../week/3.md#lets-make-a-web-page).

As with [*Manuscript*](1.md#semantic-dom), you’ll start your `index.html` with [semantic HTML](../topic/html/index.md) structures appropriate to your text and design. In this phase, we will also expect you to begin your mobile and desktop styles, using [media queries](../topic/responsive/index.md#media-queries) and [CSS variables](../topic/box-model/index.md#defined-as---variable) to deliberately structure this work—taking a [*mobile-first*](../topic/responsive/index.md#mobile-first-design) approach.

All of this work should be done synchronously *together*, as outlined above. When this is in a good spot, submit a link to the repo and URL.

You will also continue to refine your overall project design and code execution, integrating the feedback from your classmates and instructors in the previous phase. This work should also all be done *together*, as before—balancing the efforts (and commits) between both students.
